Understanding MEPCO Bill Charges

Your MEPCO bill is more than one number. It is made of the cost of the units you used, plus fuel and quarterly adjustments, plus taxes and fees. This page explains it line by line, so you can tell a genuine increase from a mistake.

Bill anatomy — line by line

Line on your billWhat it means
Previous reading / Present readingThe meter reading at the end of last month and at the end of this month. The difference is your consumption.
Units consumed (kWh)Present reading minus previous reading. This is what you are charged for.
Energy chargesYour units multiplied by the per-unit tariff rate for your category and slab.
Fixed chargesA fixed monthly charge for your connection type, if applicable to your tariff category.
FPA — Fuel Price AdjustmentAn adjustment for changes in fuel costs. It can go up or down, and it applies to units consumed in an earlier month — so it can appear even in a month when your usage was low.
QTA — Quarterly Tariff AdjustmentA quarterly adjustment to the tariff, set under NEPRA's tariff decisions. Like FPA, it is not a penalty — it is a tariff adjustment spread across bills.
Taxes and feesSee the taxes section below.
Arrears / previous balanceAny unpaid amount carried forward from earlier bills. If you paid, check it against your receipt (see bill payment).
Late payment surcharge (LPS)10% added if the previous bill was paid after its due date.
Amount payable / Due dateThe total to pay, and the last date to pay it without the surcharge.

Protected vs unprotected consumers — slab table

Domestic consumers are charged in slabs: the more units you use, the higher the per-unit rate for the higher slabs. On top of that, there is an important split — protected vs unprotected:

Consumer typeWho qualifiesHow they are charged
ProtectedDomestic consumers who used 200 units or fewer per month for the previous 6 consecutive months.Lower slab rates. If you cross 200 units in a month, you lose protected status.
UnprotectedDomestic consumers who used more than 200 units in any of the previous 6 months.Higher slab rates. You return to protected status only after 6 consecutive months at or below 200 units.

Your protected/unprotected status is re-checked every month, based on the last 6 months. That is why a single high-usage month (a hot summer month with fans and AC, for example) can raise your rate for months afterwards — not just for that one bill.

Note on rates: Per-unit slab rates change when NEPRA issues a new tariff notification. This page does not list rupee rates as current fact — check the rates printed on your own bill, or the current NEPRA tariff notification, for the month you are checking.

Taxes and fees on your bill

  • GST (General Sales Tax) — charged on your electricity bill.
  • Electricity duty — 1.5% of the bill.
  • PTV fee — Rs 35 per month.
  • Withholding tax (WHT) — 7.5% on bills over Rs 25,000 (for consumers whose name is not on the Active Taxpayers List, as applicable).

These are government charges collected through the bill — MEPCO does not keep them. If a tax line looks wrong, the correction still goes through MEPCO's complaint route (see complaint / helpline).

The most common overcharge: wrong tariff category

The single most common reason a bill is higher than it should be is a wrong tariff category — for example, a home connection billed as commercial, or a domestic connection charged at a higher category than it should be. Check the tariff/category printed on your bill against what your connection actually is. If it is wrong, take your bill and CNIC to your SDO office and ask for the category to be corrected — and keep a copy of your written request.

Check your meter reading — with a dated photo

Before complaining about a high bill, check the reading yourself:

  1. Go to your meter and read the present reading.
  2. Take a dated photo of the meter showing the reading (your phone's photo already carries the date).
  3. Compare it with the "present reading" on your bill. If the bill's reading is much higher than your meter shows, the bill was probably based on a wrong or estimated reading.
  4. Keep the photo — it is your evidence if you complain.

Other reasons for a high bill: an estimated reading (meter reader could not reach the meter), a faulty meter, or a detection bill (see complaint / helpline for detection-bill rights).

Tubewell / agricultural connections — subsidy note

Agricultural (tubewell) connections in MEPCO's area have their own tariff category and, at various times, government subsidy schemes. The rates and subsidy terms for tubewells change with government policy — check the category and rate printed on your own tubewell bill, and confirm any current subsidy at your SDO office, rather than relying on an old guide. If your tubewell bill is charged at a domestic or commercial rate, that is a category error — raise it the same way as above.

Frequently asked questions

What is FPA on my MEPCO bill?

Fuel Price Adjustment — an adjustment for changes in fuel costs. It applies to units used in an earlier month, so it can appear even when this month's usage was low.

What is QTA?

Quarterly Tariff Adjustment — a quarterly adjustment under NEPRA's tariff decisions. It is a tariff adjustment, not a penalty.

What is a protected consumer?

A domestic consumer who used 200 units or fewer per month for the previous 6 consecutive months. Protected consumers pay lower slab rates.

Why did my bill jump even though I used the same units?

Common reasons: you crossed into a higher slab, you lost protected status after a high-usage month, FPA/QTA changed, or the reading was estimated or wrong. Check the readings first, then the slab and category.

What is the Rs 35 charge on my bill?

The PTV fee — Rs 35 per month, a government charge collected through the bill.

What should I do if the meter reading on my bill is wrong?

Take a dated photo of your meter, then complain with the photo, your bill and your CNIC — see complaint / helpline.
