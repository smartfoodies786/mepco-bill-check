MEPCO Bill Complaint / Helpline

If your MEPCO bill is wrong — wrong reading, wrong tariff category, faulty meter, or a detection bill you do not accept — you have a clear route to complain. Follow the order below: most complaints are fixed at the local office, and only the ones that are not should go higher.

Keep your complaint number. Whatever route you use — phone, CCMS, SMS or in person — note the complaint/reference number, the date, and the name of the person you spoke to. Without that number, following up is very hard.

Complaint order — step by step

  1. Verify your meter first. Read your meter yourself and take a dated photo of the reading. Compare it with the present reading on your bill (see understanding bill charges). This photo is your main evidence.
  2. Go to your SDO office with your original CNIC, your bill, and the meter photo. Explain the problem in writing and get a receiving stamp / copy of your application. Ask how long the correction will take.
  3. If the SDO does not resolve it, escalate through the helpline (118 / 0800-63726) or CCMS, quoting your earlier complaint number and date.
  4. If 60 days pass with no resolution, you can take the matter to NEPRA (the power regulator). Keep all your papers — bill copies, meter photo, complaint numbers and dates — because NEPRA will ask for the full history.

Detection bills — your rights

A "detection bill" is an extra charge MEPCO adds when it believes electricity was used without being properly recorded — for example, after a meter is found tampered with or faulty. If you receive a detection bill you disagree with:

  • Ask the SDO office, in writing, for the basis of the detection — what was found, when, and how the amount was calculated.
  • Do not accept a verbal figure. A detection charge should be explained and documented.
  • Keep paying your normal current bill while the detection dispute is being examined, so your regular supply is not affected by a separate dispute — and keep all receipts.
  • If the office does not answer, follow the complaint order above, up to NEPRA after 60 days.

Disconnection timeline

If a bill is not paid by the due date, the 10% late payment surcharge applies first. Continued non-payment can lead to disconnection of supply after notice — the exact timeline depends on the notice served and the arrears involved. If your supply is disconnected, clear the dues and contact your SDO office for reconnection; confirm any reconnection fee with the office itself, in writing, before paying it.

Fake-agent warning

  • No MEPCO officer will ask you to pay a bill or a "fine" to a personal JazzCash/Easypaisa number or a personal bank account. Pay only through the official methods on your bill (see bill payment).
  • Never share an OTP, your CNIC photo, or your bank details with someone who calls claiming to be from MEPCO. MEPCO does not ask for these on the phone.
  • Beware of fake apps and fake "MEPCO" numbers on WhatsApp. Use only the official portal (bill.pitc.com.pk/mepcobill), the numbers on this page, or a MEPCO office in person.
  • If someone threatens immediate disconnection unless you pay at once to a personal number, it is almost certainly a scam. Hang up and call 118 yourself to check your account status.

Frequently asked questions

What is MEPCO's helpline number?

118 (national helpline) and 0800-63726 (MEPCO toll-free). Head office: 061-9220313/14/17.

How do I complain about a wrong MEPCO bill?

First verify your meter and take a dated photo, then go to your SDO office with your CNIC, bill and photo. If it is not resolved, escalate via 118/CCMS, and after 60 days you can approach NEPRA.

What is CCMS?

The online Complaint Management System at ccms.pitc.com.pk (email ccms@pitc.com.pk). File your complaint there and keep the complaint number.

Can I complain by SMS?

Yes — 8118 is the complaints/alerts SMS short code.

What is a detection bill?

An extra charge added when MEPCO believes electricity was used without being properly recorded. Ask the SDO office in writing for the basis and calculation of the charge.

Someone called asking me to pay a fine to a personal number. Is it real?

No — that is a scam. MEPCO never asks for payment to a personal mobile wallet or account, and never asks for your OTP or bank details on the phone. Call 118 yourself to check.
